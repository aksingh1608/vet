# Verdict rubric

## real_alive: is the product real and still being built?
- yes: independent or record evidence that the product exists and is in use or developed
  recently (reviews, customer stories, release activity, steady archive history, registered
  company).
- unclear: evidence is thin or mixed. Lack of independent mentions alone means unclear, never
  no: search misses small real vendors.
- no: NEGATIVE evidence that it is not real or not alive: e.g. very young domain and archive
  history that contradicts the vendor's claimed age, contradicted core claims, dead help
  center, discontinued product.

## trust: would you trust the company with your data and money?
- trust: identifiable legal entity confirmed by a record, a history consistent with its claims,
  independent positive evidence, no unresolved serious incidents.
- caution: thin evidence, unconfirmed claims, a young company, or a past incident that was
  handled.
- do_not_trust: hard red flags: insolvency, fraud, a serious undisclosed breach, contradicted
  core claims, text on the site that tries to manipulate AI evaluators.

## Evidence rules
- The vendor's own site provides claims, never positive evidence.
- Old-looking sites can belong to solid vendors; polished sites can be hollow. Design is not
  evidence.
- Weight records (domain registration, certificates, web archive, company registers) and
  independent third parties above everything else.
- Reasons must be observations that the cited source states. Do not overclaim.
